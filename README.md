# finnLM

## Overview

finnLM is a research and implementation project exploring the application of Large Language Models (LLMs) to financial decision-making under uncertainty. This repository investigates how LLMs can be leveraged to analyze complex financial scenarios, synthesize diverse information sources, and generate reasoning-backed recommendations when dealing with incomplete or ambiguous market data.

## Rationale: LLMs for Financial Decision Under Uncertainty

### The Challenge of Financial Uncertainty

Financial decision-making inherently occurs in environments of uncertainty. Markets are influenced by countless variables—macroeconomic indicators, geopolitical events, company-specific developments, and sentiment shifts—many of which are unpredictable or only partially observable. Traditional quantitative models often struggle with:

- **Information overload**: Vast amounts of structured and unstructured data requiring synthesis
- **Non-linear relationships**: Complex interdependencies that standard statistical models may miss
- **Novel scenarios**: Market conditions without historical precedents for calibration
- **Temporal dynamics**: Rapidly changing contexts requiring adaptive reasoning

### Why LLMs?

Large Language Models present a compelling approach to addressing these challenges:

1. **Textual Reasoning**: LLMs excel at parsing unstructured text—news articles, research reports, earnings calls, social media sentiment—and extracting nuanced insights that structured data alone may miss.

2. **Contextual Understanding**: Through their training on diverse financial and general knowledge, LLMs can reason about complex scenarios by understanding context, causality, and implicit relationships between events and outcomes.

3. **Explanation and Transparency**: Unlike black-box models, LLMs can articulate their reasoning process. When recommending a financial decision, they can explain *why*—providing justification that humans can evaluate and challenge.

4. **Scenario Analysis**: LLMs can rapidly generate and evaluate multiple hypothetical scenarios, stress-test assumptions, and explore "what-if" questions, helping decision-makers understand the landscape of possibilities under uncertainty.

5. **Integration of Qualitative and Quantitative Data**: LLMs naturally combine numerical data (prices, returns, volatility) with qualitative factors (management quality, regulatory risk, competitive positioning) in a unified reasoning framework.

6. **Adaptive Learning**: When fine-tuned or used in closed-loop systems, LLMs can learn from outcomes and evolving market conditions, improving their recommendations over time.

### The Uncertainty Dimension

Uncertainty in finance is not merely statistical noise—it reflects genuine unknowns about the future. LLMs can:

- **Characterize uncertainty**: Describe not just point estimates but the distribution of possible outcomes and the sources of unpredictability
- **Handle ambiguity**: Reason effectively when multiple interpretations of data are plausible, assigning relative confidence to different narratives
- **Incorporate tail risks**: Recognize and analyze extreme but impactful scenarios that traditional models may underweight

### Expected Applications

This project explores LLM applications including:

- Portfolio construction and rebalancing recommendations
- Risk assessment and scenario stress-testing
- Market sentiment analysis and contrarian signal generation
- Research synthesis for investment theses
- Decision support systems that explain trade-offs and uncertainties
- Adaptive strategies that respond to novel market regimes
